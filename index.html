CRIMINAL CASE • GRÖBERN, BAVARIA • 31.03.1922

Hinter—kaifeck

The killer lived on the farm with the bodies of his victims. He ate their bread. Fed their cattle. Kept the stove burning — until the neighbors looked into the barn.

Six people were killed with a mattock in a single night. Yet smoke kept rising from the chimney for three more days. This is a story not only about a murder — but about someone who knew the house, the attic and every habit of the family from the inside.

01 — What happened

The house everyone
walked past

Hinterkaifeck stood apart from the village of Gröbern, right at the edge of the forest. The Gruber family lived in seclusion and had a reputation for being strange: the owner Andreas was harsh, barely spoke to the neighbors, and dark rumors had circulated for years about his relationship with his daughter Viktoria.

On the evening of March 31, 1922, four members of the family — one after another — were lured into the barn. There they were killed with blows of a mattock. Then the killer entered the house, where he killed two-year-old Josef in his crib and the new maid Maria Baumgartner, who had arrived at the farm only a few hours earlier.

“Footprints led from the forest to the house.
There were none leading back.”

The most terrifying part came after. On Sunday, the family was not seen at church. Neighbors noticed smoke from the chimney, heard that the cattle had been fed, and heard the dog barking from the locked barn. Someone kept living the ordinary life of the farm — next to six bodies.

05 — Chronology

From footprints in the snow — to silence

AUTUMN 1921

The maid leaves

The previous maid resigns after night-time sounds and the feeling that the house is being watched. Her account will later become the first page of the case file.

A FEW DAYS BEFORE

Footprints with no way back

Andreas finds footprints in the snow: from the forest to the house. Not a single one leads back. Yet no stranger is found in the house.

MARCH 1922

A newspaper, keys, the attic

A key disappears, a strange newspaper appears, and footsteps and shuffling are heard in the attic at night. Andreas refuses to go to the police and will not let anyone stay overnight to keep watch.

MARCH 31 • DAY

Maria arrives

Forty-four-year-old Maria Baumgartner arrives for her new job. Her sister would later say she seemed to sense the coming disaster and did not want to go.

MARCH 31 • EVENING

Four walk into the barn

Andreas, the elder Cäzilia, Viktoria and seven-year-old Cäzilia are killed in the barn one after another. The bodies are laid side by side and covered with hay and an old door.

MARCH 31 • NIGHT

The killer enters the house

Maria is killed in her room, by the doorway. Two-year-old Josef dies in his mother’s bedroom. The money and valuables in the house are mostly left in place.

APRIL 1–3

Smoke over the empty house

Neighbors see smoke from the chimney. The cattle are fed, the cows stop lowing, the postman leaves the mail. The family is absent from Sunday church — and this does not immediately make anyone go inside.

APRIL 4

The neighbors open the barn

Alarmed by the family’s absence, the neighbors led by Lorenz Schlittenbauer enter the farm and find the bodies. The police will arrive later — to a crime scene already disturbed.

06 — The most chilling part
0HOURS

He didn’t run away.
He stayed and lived.

This is not proven by direct testimony — it is a conclusion drawn from the neighbors’ observations and the examination of the house. But it is precisely what makes Hinterkaifeck a legend of criminology.

FACT FROM THE NEIGHBORS’ ACCOUNTS

Smoke from the chimney was noticed after the presumed night of the murders.

FACT OF THE EXAMINATION

Freshly cut bread and signs of a recent meal were found in the house, and the cattle had been fed.

THE KEY QUESTION

Why would the killer risk staying where the neighbors could walk in at any moment?

08 — Why the case failed

The evidence was destroyed.
The farm was demolished.

A century’s investigation — with the methods of its century

01

Neighbors and onlookers entered the crime scene before the police and moved objects around.

02

The victims’ heads were sent to clairvoyants in Munich. The skulls were later lost during the Second World War.

03

Suspects were interrogated for years, but the key checks were carried out too late.

04

As early as 1923 the farm was demolished. Items missed during the first examination were found in the rubble.

Today, the site of Hinterkaifeck is a field with a memorial stone. In 2007, police academy students re-examined the files with modern methods, but the name of the presumed culprit was never published: guilt cannot be proven a century later.
